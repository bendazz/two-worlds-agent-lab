THE DRIFT REMEMBERED

by Tamsin Vey, Rope-Scribe of Aubade

CHAPTER 1: PREFACE

This book is a description and a history of the Kestrine Drift. The Kestrine Drift is a scattering of islands that float above a sea of cloud. The people who live there call their home simply the Drift, and this book does the same. The book was set down by Tamsin Vey, Rope-Scribe of Aubade, in the year AL 340.

The Drift counts its years with the AL calendar. The letters AL stand for "After the Lowering." The Lowering was the time when the islands of the Drift stopped their rise and settled in the clouds. The first year of the settled islands is AL 1, and every year since is counted from it. The time before the Lowering is called the Rising, and the Rising has no numbered years at all.

A Rope-Scribe is a keeper of records in the Cord Archive of Aubade. For most of the Drift's history, records were not written in ink. Records were tied in knotted cord, and a Rope-Scribe must read a knotted cord as easily as a written page. Tamsin Vey has served as a Rope-Scribe of the Cord Archive since AL 322. This book draws on three sources: the knot-records of the Cord Archive, the rope-songs of the Pennons, and the accounts that the people of each island gave to the author during a year of travel along the bridges and the Long Tether.

This book is arranged in three parts. Part One describes the islands of the Drift one at a time: Kestra, Orrin's Shelf with the rock called Orrin's Step, the Pennons, Salt Kettle, and Low Drift. Part Two tells the history of the Drift in six eras: the Rising, the Settling, the Salt War, the Knotted Years, the Great Gale, and the Rebinding. Part Three describes the notable figures of the Drift. Two appendices close the book. Appendix A is a timeline, and Appendix B is a glossary of the words a visitor to the Drift will hear.

A reader should know what this book does not contain. This book does not describe what lies beneath the clouds, because no one in the Drift knows. The people of the Drift are afraid of heights. No one in the Drift looks over the edge of an island, and no knot-record in the Cord Archive describes the view downward. Where the Cord Archive is silent, this book is silent too.

A reader should also know how this book treats numbers. The Cord Archive records dates, prices, and counts with great care, and this book copies them as the Archive gives them. Where the Archive gives a year, this book gives the year. Where the Archive does not give a number, this book does not guess one.

The author thanks the Council of Aubade, the bridge-wardens of the Pennons, the Brinewarden of Salt Kettle, the terrace farmers of Orrin's Shelf, and the stone-cutters of Low Drift, all of whom answered questions with patience. Any knot tied wrongly in this book is the author's own.
PART ONE: THE ISLANDS

CHAPTER 2: KESTRA

Kestra is the largest island of the Kestrine Drift. The Drift takes its name from Kestra, for the old word "Kestrine" means "of Kestra." Kestra lies near the middle of the Drift, and the people of the Drift describe every other island by where it lies from Kestra. A walker who keeps well back from the edges can cross Kestra from its western brink to its eastern brink in one long day.

Kestra is the island of the weavers. The weavers of Kestra make the cord, rope, and cloth that hold the Drift together, and without Kestran rope there would be no bridges and no Long Tether. The raw material of Kestran weaving is tetherweed, a tall grey-green plant that grows in the damp fields of Kestra's interior. Tetherweed is cut in late summer, soaked in cistern water, beaten flat, and spun into thread. The weavers of Kestra say that a good weaver can tell the age of a rope from the smell of its fibers.

Kestra has two great weaving villages, Thrum and Wefting. Thrum lies on the western side of Kestra and makes heavy rope, including the thick ropes used for bridges. Wefting lies on the eastern side of Kestra and makes fine cord and cloth. Wefting is the only place in the Drift where Council cord, the cord used for money, may be woven. The weavers of Wefting weave Council cord from red and grey thread twisted together, and any person who weaves red-and-grey cord outside Wefting breaks the law of the Council.

Aubade is the capital of the Kestrine Drift, and Aubade stands on Kestra. Aubade is built on a low rise near the center of Kestra, as far from every edge as the island allows. The houses of Aubade are built of grey cloudstone carried up from Low Drift, and their roofs are thatched with tetherweed stalks. The city of Aubade has three quarters: the Loom Quarter, where the weavers' halls stand; the Cistern Terrace, where the great water tanks are kept; and the Hall Quarter, where the Council meets.

The Council of Aubade governs the Kestrine Drift. The Council of Aubade is an elected council, and it is led by a First Speaker. The Kestrine Drift has no monarch. The Drift has never had a king or a queen, and the people of the Drift regard the idea of a ruler by birth as a strange and foolish one. Every councillor is elected by the people of that councillor's own island, and the councillors then elect a First Speaker from among themselves.

The Council of Aubade has fifteen seats in AL 340. Kestra holds six seats, Orrin's Shelf holds three seats, the Pennons hold three seats, Salt Kettle holds two seats, and Low Drift holds one seat. A First Speaker is elected for a term of nine years and may be elected again. The First Speaker calls the Council together, speaks for the Drift when the islands must speak with one voice, and casts the deciding vote when the councillors are evenly split.

The Council of Aubade meets in the Hall of Cords. The Hall of Cords is a long cloudstone building in the Hall Quarter of Aubade, and its ceiling is hung with thousands of knotted cords. Each cord in the Hall of Cords is a law of the Drift, tied in knots by a Rope-Scribe on the day the Council passed it. When a law is repealed, its cord is cut down and moved to the Cord Archive, which stands beside the Hall of Cords.

The Kestrine Drift has no rivers. No river or stream flows on Kestra or on any other island of the Drift. All the water in the Drift comes from rain, and the rain is caught in cisterns. The largest cistern in the Drift is the Great Cistern of Aubade, a covered tank of cloudstone on the Cistern Terrace. Every village on Kestra has at least one cistern, and every roof in Aubade is shaped to send rainwater into a cistern pipe.

The cisterns of the Drift are guarded by rain-keepers. A rain-keeper measures the water in a cistern each morning, opens and closes the cistern pipes, and decides how much water each household may draw in a dry season. The oldest water law in the Hall of Cords forbids any person to soak tetherweed in a drinking cistern. The rain-keepers of Kestra are elected by their villages, and a rain-keeper cannot be removed except by a vote of the village that chose them.

The money of the Kestrine Drift is cord. The smallest unit of money is the knot, which is a measured length of Council cord tied off at both ends. Twenty knots make one spool. Six spools make one bolt. Knots are used for small purchases, spools for larger ones, and bolts for trade between islands. In AL 340, a loaf of lichen-barley bread in the markets of Aubade costs one knot.

The edges of Kestra are called brinks, and every brink of Kestra is walled. The brink walls of Kestra are built of cloudstone at the bottom and woven tetherweed screens at the top, so that no person standing near a brink can see past it. The people of Kestra, like all the people of the Drift, are afraid of heights, and no Kestran looks over the edge of the island. Kestran parents teach their children a short saying before any other: "Eyes inward."

No one in the Kestrine Drift flies. The Drift has no winged beasts that carry riders, no balloons, and no flying machines, and the people of the Drift would not use such things if they had them. The people of the Drift travel between islands in only two ways: on foot along rope bridges, and in cable-barges that hang from great cables. On Kestra itself, people travel on foot or in carts pulled by hand.

The Long Tether is the great cable that runs between Kestra and Orrin's Shelf. The Kestran end of the Long Tether is fixed to the Tether-post, a pillar of cloudstone on the northern brink of Kestra. Cable-barges leave from the Tether-post every morning and every afternoon. The Tether-post is the busiest place on the northern side of Kestra, because every sack of lichen-barley eaten in Aubade passes through it.

Kestra holds the largest share of the Drift's people. The Cord Archive does not record an exact count of the people of Kestra, but the Council knot-records show that more households draw water from the cisterns of Kestra than from the cisterns of all the other islands together. The festival most loved on Kestra is the Night of Loose Ends, held on the last night of each year, when every household unties one old knot and ties one new one for the coming year.
CHAPTER 3: ORRIN'S SHELF AND ORRIN'S STEP

Orrin's Shelf is the island of the terrace farmers. Orrin's Shelf lies to the north of Kestra, and Orrin's Shelf is tilted, so that its whole surface slopes gently down from its high western rim to its low eastern rim. The farmers of Orrin's Shelf have cut this slope into nine long terraces, one below the next, each held up by a wall of fitted stone. Seen from the Tether-landing, the terraces of Orrin's Shelf look like the steps of a great staircase laid on its side.

Orrin's Shelf grows lichen-barley, and lichen-barley is the only grain grown anywhere in the Kestrine Drift. Lichen-barley is a short, tough grain with pale silver husks. Lichen-barley grows well in the thin, cool air of the Shelf, and it will not grow on Kestra, the Pennons, Salt Kettle, or Low Drift, though the people of those islands have tried many times. Every loaf of bread, every bowl of porridge, and every cup of ale in the Drift begins on the terraces of Orrin's Shelf.

Orrin's Shelf takes its name from Orrin Kell, the farmer who cut the first terrace. The knot-records of the Cord Archive say that Orrin Kell cut the first terrace on the Shelf in AL 3, using a stone blade and a basket for carrying soil. The first terrace, now called Kell's Terrace, is the highest of the nine terraces and is still farmed today. The farmers of Orrin's Shelf leave one furrow of Kell's Terrace unplanted every year in memory of Orrin Kell.

The terrace farmers of Orrin's Shelf live in the village of Furrowend, which stands at the low eastern end of the terraces. Furrowend is built of stone rather than cloudstone, because the Shelf has its own grey rock. The farmers of Furrowend water their terraces from cisterns cut into the terrace walls, and every terrace has its own cistern and its own rain-keeper. Like every island of the Drift, Orrin's Shelf has no river, and the harvest of the Shelf depends entirely on the rain.

The harvest of Orrin's Shelf ends with Sheaf Night. Sheaf Night is held on the night the last sheaf of lichen-barley is cut, and the date changes from year to year. On Sheaf Night the farmers of Orrin's Shelf light lamps along every terrace wall, bake the first bread of the new harvest, and send the first loaf down the Long Tether to the Council of Aubade as a gift.

Orrin's Shelf keeps its grain in the Deep Granary. The Deep Granary is a set of stone cellars dug into the rock beneath Kell's Terrace. The Deep Granary can hold enough lichen-barley to feed the people of Orrin's Shelf for two winters. The farmers of Orrin's Shelf keep the Deep Granary at least half full at all times, by a law of their own that is older than the Council.

Orrin's Shelf holds three seats on the Council of Aubade. The councillors of Orrin's Shelf are always farmers, by custom, and they speak for the price of grain in every Council debate. The Shelf farmers sell their lichen-barley by the sack, and in AL 340 one sack of lichen-barley sells at the Tether-post of Kestra for three spools.

The only way to reach Orrin's Shelf is by cable-barge along the Long Tether. The Long Tether is a great cable that runs between Kestra and Orrin's Shelf, and cable-barges travel along it. A cable-barge is a covered wooden car that hangs beneath the Long Tether from iron wheels. The crew of a cable-barge winds the car along the Long Tether by turning a great crank, and the journey from Kestra to Orrin's Shelf takes most of a day. The windows of a cable-barge face upward and sideways, never downward, so that no passenger need see what lies below.

The cable-barges of the Long Tether land at the Tether-landing, a stone platform on the high western rim of Orrin's Shelf. From the Tether-landing, grain is carried up to the cable-barges in sacks and people walk down the terraces to Furrowend. No bridge of any kind joins Orrin's Shelf to any other island. If the Long Tether is cut, Orrin's Shelf is cut off from the rest of the Drift.

ORRIN'S STEP

Orrin's Step is a small rock below Orrin's Shelf, and Orrin's Step is a prison. Orrin's Step is not a part of Orrin's Shelf, and no farming is done there. Orrin's Step is a bare grey rock that floats a short distance beneath the low eastern rim of Orrin's Shelf, close enough that a person on the lowest terrace of the Shelf could throw a stone onto it. Nothing grows on Orrin's Step.

Orrin's Step has been the prison of the Kestrine Drift since AL 40. The Council of Aubade chose Orrin's Step as the prison of the Drift because the rock cannot be left without passing through Orrin's Shelf, and Orrin's Shelf cannot be left without the Long Tether. The people of the Drift consider Orrin's Step the most frightening place in the Drift, because it lies below another island and because its prisoners must live closer to the edge than anyone else.

Orrin's Step is reached from Orrin's Shelf by the Cage Ladder. The Cage Ladder is a ladder of rope and wood enclosed on every side by a woven cage, so that a person climbing it sees only the weave around them. The Cage Ladder hangs from the lowest terrace of Orrin's Shelf down to Orrin's Step. Prisoners are brought down the Cage Ladder blindfolded, and most prisoners never climb it again until their sentence ends.

The keeper of Orrin's Step is called the Step-keeper. The Step-keeper is appointed by the Council of Aubade and lives on Orrin's Shelf, not on Orrin's Step. The Step-keeper sends food down the Cage Ladder each morning in baskets, and the prisoners of Orrin's Step are fed on lichen-barley porridge and cistern water. Orrin's Step has stone cells for no more than thirty prisoners, and the Cord Archive records that Orrin's Step has never held more than thirty prisoners at one time.

The prisoners of Orrin's Step spend their days twisting tetherweed thread into rough cord, which the Step-keeper sends up the Cage Ladder and sells on Kestra. The most famous prisoner ever held on Orrin's Step was Harl Quenby, the Brinewarden of Salt Kettle who began the Salt War. The story of Harl Quenby is told in the chapter on the Salt War.

A visitor must take care not to confuse Orrin's Shelf with Orrin's Step. Orrin's Shelf is the farming island of the nine terraces, the home of lichen-barley and the village of Furrowend. Orrin's Step is the small prison rock below it. The people of the Drift have a saying for a person who has fallen from good fortune to bad: "gone from the Shelf to the Step."
CHAPTER 4: THE PENNONS

The Pennons are a chain of forty tiny islands joined by rope bridges. The Pennons curve away from Kestra toward the south-west in a long, bending line, and each Pennon is joined to the next by a rope bridge. The largest of the Pennons is smaller than the smallest village of Kestra, and the smallest of the Pennons holds only a single house and a cistern. Together, the forty Pennons are home to more people than any island of the Drift except Kestra.

The Pennons are numbered rather than named. The Pennon nearest to Kestra is called the First Pennon, the next is called the Second Pennon, and so on along the chain to the Fortieth Pennon at the far south-western end. The people of the Pennons give their home island by its number, so that a Pennon family might say they are "of the Twelfth." The old rope-songs say that the Pennons once had names, but that the names were forgotten during the Rising and the numbers were given during the Settling.

The Pennons are joined to Kestra by the Kestra Span. The Kestra Span is a rope bridge that runs from the First Pennon to the south-western brink of Kestra. The Kestra Span is the longest rope bridge in the Drift, and crossing the Kestra Span on foot takes about an hour at a steady walk. Every person, basket, and cart that travels between the Pennons and Kestra crosses the Kestra Span.

The rope bridges of the Pennons are built for people who are afraid of heights. Each bridge in the Pennons has a floor of wooden slats laid on four heavy ropes, and each bridge has woven sides of tetherweed cloth as tall as a grown person. The woven sides of a Pennon bridge are laced to a woven roof, so that a bridge in the Pennons is really a long cloth tunnel hung between two islands. A traveler on a Pennon bridge sees only cloth and the floor ahead, and never the drop below. The people of the Pennons say that a bridge without sides is not a bridge but a dare.

The rope bridges of the Pennons are kept by the bridge-wardens. A bridge-warden inspects the ropes of a bridge every morning, replaces worn slats, and reties any knot that has begun to slip. Every bridge in the Pennons has at least one bridge-warden, and the Kestra Span has twelve. The bridge-wardens of the Pennons belong to the Guild of Wardens, which was founded in AL 120 and which trains every new warden for four years before the warden may tie a load-bearing knot.

The bridge-wardens of the Pennons use a knot called the warden's hitch for every load-bearing join. The warden's hitch can be untied only by a bridge-warden who knows the correct order of its turns, and teaching the warden's hitch to anyone outside the Guild of Wardens is forbidden. Each bridge-warden carries a short cord called a tally-string, and ties one knot in it for every bridge inspection. When a warden's tally-string is full, the warden hangs it in the Guild Hall on the Ninth Pennon.

The people of the Pennons pay for their bridges with tolls. Every traveler who crosses the Kestra Span pays a toll of one knot to the bridge-wardens at the First Pennon. Travelers who cross between two Pennons pay nothing, because the Pennon families pay their own share of the bridge-wardens' keep each year. The tolls of the Kestra Span are counted in the Guild Hall on the Ninth Pennon and pay for new rope from Thrum.

The people of the Pennons live by small trades. There is no room on the Pennons for fields, so each Pennon family keeps a few hens in a woven coop and grows herbs and greens in stone boxes along the walls of their house. The people of the Pennons are known across the Drift as makers of baskets, sellers of eggs, and carriers of goods. Much of the carrying in the Drift is done by Pennon porters, who walk goods from island to island along the bridges and are paid by the load.

The Pennons are the home of the rope-songs. A rope-song is a song sung by bridge-wardens and porters while they work, keeping time with the pull of a rope or the fall of a foot on a slat. Many rope-songs are very old and remember things from the Rising and the Settling that are recorded nowhere else. The Cord Archive of Aubade keeps a record of more than four hundred rope-songs, each tied as a knot-record of its words.

The most famous poet of the Kestrine Drift came from the Pennons. The poet Liesl Marro came from the Twelfth Pennon, and Liesl Marro's great work, Songs from the Rope, gathered the rope-songs of the Pennons into a single book of poems. Songs from the Rope is still recited on the Pennons on the first evening of every year. The life of Liesl Marro is told in the chapter on notable figures.

The Pennons hold three seats on the Council of Aubade. The three Pennon councillors are chosen by a vote of all forty Pennons together, and by custom one of the three councillors is always a bridge-warden. The Pennon councillors are known for voting as a single bloc, because the Pennons depend on one another more closely than any other islands of the Drift. A saying of the Pennons puts it plainly: "Cut one bridge and forty families fall silent."

The Pennons suffer more than any other part of the Drift from high winds. The Pennons are small and light, and in a strong wind the whole chain sways, so that the bridges between the Pennons creak and stretch. Every Pennon house is built low and round, with its door facing away from the prevailing wind. The worst wind in the history of the Pennons was the Great Gale of AL 214, which is described in its own chapter.
CHAPTER 5: SALT KETTLE

Salt Kettle is the island of the salt-boilers. Salt Kettle holds the only brine spring in the Kestrine Drift, and every grain of salt eaten anywhere in the Drift comes from Salt Kettle. Salt Kettle is a round, bowl-shaped island that lies at the far south-western end of the Drift. The island is small, but because of its salt, Salt Kettle has always been one of the most important islands of the Drift.

The brine spring of Salt Kettle is called the Kettle. The Kettle rises in the lowest point of the bowl at the center of Salt Kettle, and the water of the Kettle is warm, cloudy, and very salty. Steam rises from the Kettle on cold mornings, and the island takes its name from that steam, which makes the whole bowl look like a pot on a fire. No other spring of any kind has ever been found in the Drift. The Kettle is the only water in the Drift that does not come from rain.

The people of Salt Kettle make salt by boiling brine. The salt-boilers of Salt Kettle draw brine from the Kettle in buckets, pour it into wide, shallow iron pans, and boil the brine over fires until only salt is left. The salt is scraped from the pans, dried in the sun, and pressed into flat round cakes. A cake of Kettle salt is about the size of a person's palm, and every cake is stamped with the mark of the salt-boiler who made it.

The salt-boilers of Salt Kettle burn lamp-moss in their fires. Lamp-moss is a dense, oily moss that grows in the damp shadows of Low Drift, and it burns slowly with a steady flame. The salt-boilers of Salt Kettle buy their lamp-moss from Low Drift by the bale. Without lamp-moss the pans of Salt Kettle could not be kept boiling, and the salt-boilers say that every cake of salt is half brine and half moss.

The salt-boilers of Salt Kettle train their apprentices for seven years. A salt-boiler's apprentice spends the first years of training only carrying buckets from the Kettle to the pans, and does not touch a pan-scraper until the fourth year. At the end of the seventh year, the apprentice is given a boiler's mark of their own to stamp on their salt cakes. The marks of the salt-boilers of Salt Kettle are recorded in the Cord Archive of Aubade, and no two salt-boilers may share a mark.

The only town on Salt Kettle is Seethe. Seethe is built in rings around the rim of the bowl of Salt Kettle, with the boiling-houses on the inner rings nearest the Kettle and the homes on the outer rings. The houses of Seethe are white with salt crust, and visitors often say that Seethe looks as if it were carved from a single block of salt. The salt-boilers of Seethe work in shifts so that some pans are boiling at every hour of the day and night.

Salt Kettle drinks rain, like every other island of the Drift. The water of the Kettle is far too salty to drink, so the people of Seethe catch rain in cisterns on the outer rings of the town, well away from the brine. The Kettle is a spring and not a river, and no stream runs from it: the brine of the Kettle rises into its bowl and stays there until the salt-boilers draw it. The rain-keepers of Seethe guard their cisterns closely, because a single bucket of brine spilled into a drinking cistern would spoil it for a season.

Salt Kettle is governed on its own affairs by the Brinewarden. The Brinewarden of Salt Kettle is chosen by the salt-boilers of Seethe and keeps the Kettle, sets the day's boiling, and decides how much salt leaves the island. The Brinewarden has always been the most powerful person on Salt Kettle, because whoever controls the Kettle controls the salt of the whole Drift. The title of Brinewarden is older than the Council of Aubade.

Salt matters in the Drift because salt keeps food. The people of the Drift salt their eggs, their greens, and their bread-dough, and in the cold months they depend on salted food laid by in the harvest season. Without salt, the food of the Drift spoils within days. The knot-records of the Cord Archive show that in any year when salt was scarce, hunger followed in the next winter.

Salt Kettle is joined to the rest of the Drift by the Brine Bridge. The Brine Bridge is a rope bridge that runs from the north-eastern rim of Salt Kettle to the Fortieth Pennon. The Brine Bridge is the only bridge that touches Salt Kettle, and every cake of salt that leaves Salt Kettle is carried across the Brine Bridge. Like the bridges of the Pennons, the Brine Bridge has woven sides and a woven roof, so that no traveler crossing it sees below.

The middle of the Brine Bridge is called the Midspan. The Midspan is a wide wooden platform hung at the center of the Brine Bridge, broad enough for a dozen people to stand on together. The Midspan was built so that the salt-boilers of Salt Kettle and the porters of the Pennons could meet halfway, trade salt for goods, and each return home without setting foot on the other's island. The Midspan is also the place where the Tether Compact was signed, as the chapter on the Salt War describes.

Salt Kettle holds two seats on the Council of Aubade. Salt Kettle did not hold any seats on the Council from the founding of the Council until the end of the Salt War. The two seats of Salt Kettle were granted by the Tether Compact in AL 101. By custom, one of the two councillors of Salt Kettle is always the Brinewarden or a person the Brinewarden names.

The salt of Salt Kettle is sold by the cake. In AL 340, one cake of Kettle salt sells in the markets of Aubade for two spools. The price of Kettle salt is set each year by the Council of Aubade and the Brinewarden together, as the Tether Compact requires. Neither the Council nor the Brinewarden may change the price of salt without the other's agreement.

The people of Salt Kettle hold one festival that is theirs alone, the Cooling. The Cooling is held on the one day each year when every pan on Salt Kettle is left cold and the fires are put out. On the day of the Cooling, the salt-boilers of Seethe clean their pans, mend their boiling-houses, and eat unsalted bread to remind themselves what the Drift would taste like without them.
CHAPTER 6: LOW DRIFT

Low Drift is the name given to the islands that sank lowest at the Lowering. Low Drift is a cluster of seven small islands that float close together beneath the southern brink of Kestra. When the islands of the Drift stopped their rise and settled in the clouds, most of the islands dipped only a little before they came to rest. The seven islands of Low Drift dipped much farther than the rest, and they came to rest lower in the clouds than any other island of the Drift.

Low Drift is the island group of the stone-cutters. The stone-cutters of Low Drift quarry cloudstone, the pale grey stone used for building across the whole Drift. Cloudstone is soft when first cut and hardens as it dries, so the stone-cutters of Low Drift shape each block in the quarry and let it dry for a season before it is carried away. The houses of Aubade, the Great Cistern, the Hall of Cords, and the Tether-post of Kestra are all built of cloudstone from Low Drift.

The people of the Drift are afraid of heights, and the people of Low Drift are the most afraid of all. No one in the Drift looks over the edge of an island, and on Low Drift the rule is kept more strictly than anywhere else. The houses of Low Drift are built with no windows on the sides that face an edge. The quarries of Low Drift are cut downward into the middle of each island, never outward toward the brinks. A stone-cutter of Low Drift who is seen walking toward an edge without cause is stopped by the other stone-cutters and led back.

The people of Low Drift have a word, "brinkward," for anything that faces an edge, and to call a person brinkward is an insult on Low Drift. A brinkward person is a careless or foolish person. Every Low Drift child learns early that a brinkward house is a bad house, a brinkward quarry is a bad quarry, and a brinkward glance is the worst thing of all.

Low Drift is joined to Kestra by the Walled Stair. The Walled Stair is an enclosed stairway of rope and wooden treads that hangs from the southern brink of Kestra down to the largest island of Low Drift. The Walled Stair is enclosed on every side by woven tetherweed walls, and it turns back and forth on itself many times, so that a person climbing it sees only the next few treads. Climbing the Walled Stair from Low Drift to Kestra takes about two hours for a person carrying nothing.

Cloudstone does not travel up the Walled Stair. Cloudstone is far too heavy to carry up a stair, so the stone-cutters of Low Drift raise their blocks to Kestra with the Stone Hoist. The Stone Hoist is a great wooden wheel on the southern brink of Kestra, turned by teams of workers, that lifts cloudstone blocks up from Low Drift in a closed wooden box. The Stone Hoist carries only stone and never people, by a rule that the stone-cutters say is older than anyone can remember.

The largest island of Low Drift is called the Anvil, and the main settlement of Low Drift is the village of Chisel, which stands on the Anvil. Chisel is built entirely of cloudstone and is the only village in the Drift with no tetherweed roofs, because the stone-cutters roof their houses with thin cloudstone slabs. The other six islands of Low Drift are joined to the Anvil by short rope bridges with woven sides, and each of the six is given over mainly to quarries.

Low Drift is also the home of lamp-moss. Lamp-moss grows in the damp, shaded hollows of the Low Drift islands, where cloud mist gathers most thickly. Lamp-moss is cut, pressed into bales, and dried, and dry lamp-moss burns slowly with a steady yellow flame. The people of the Drift burn lamp-moss for light and heat in every island, and the salt-boilers of Salt Kettle burn it under their pans. Lamp-moss grows nowhere in the Drift except Low Drift.

Low Drift is damper and colder than any other part of the Drift. Because Low Drift sits lowest in the clouds, mist lies on the seven islands most mornings, and the sun reaches them later in the day than it reaches Kestra. The cisterns of Low Drift fill more quickly than the cisterns of any other island, because the mist leaves water on every stone surface. The rain-keepers of Low Drift are the only rain-keepers in the Drift who have never known a dry season.

Low Drift holds one seat on the Council of Aubade. Low Drift did not hold a seat on the Council until AL 163, when the Council voted to seat a councillor from Low Drift. Before AL 163, the stone-cutters of Low Drift were spoken for on the Council by the councillors of Kestra. The single councillor of Low Drift is chosen by the stone-cutters of Chisel.

The stone-cutters of Low Drift mark every block of cloudstone with a cutter's sign, a small shape carved into one face of the block. A cutter's sign tells which stone-cutter shaped the block and which quarry it came from. The cutter's signs on the walls of the Hall of Cords have been read by the Rope-Scribes of the Cord Archive, and they show that the stone of the Hall of Cords came from four different quarries of Low Drift.

The chief celebration of Low Drift is the Clearing, held each year on the first morning when the mist lifts from all seven islands at once. The morning of the Clearing cannot be known in advance, and the stone-cutters of Low Drift wait for it with lamps lit. On the morning of the Clearing, work stops in every quarry and the people of Chisel eat a breakfast of lichen-barley porridge together in the open.
PART TWO: THE HISTORY

CHAPTER 7: THE RISING

The Rising is the name the people of the Drift give to the time before the Lowering. During the Rising, the islands of the Drift were still climbing upward through the clouds. The Rising has no numbered years, because the AL calendar begins only when the Rising ended. The Cord Archive holds no knot-record from the Rising, and everything this chapter says about the Rising comes from the old rope-songs of the Pennons.

The rope-songs say that people already lived on the islands during the Rising. The rope-songs do not say where those people came from or how they first reached the islands, and this book does not guess. The rope-songs say only that the people of the Rising lived on islands that never stayed still, and that each year the islands climbed a little higher and the air grew a little thinner.

Life during the Rising was hard. The rope-songs say that during the Rising the islands drifted apart and together again with every season, so that no bridge could be built that would not snap within a year. The people of each island lived apart from the people of every other island, and they could trade only when the islands happened to drift close together. The oldest rope-songs call this time "the years of the loose rope."

The rope-songs say that the people of the Rising had no council and no leaders chosen by vote. Each island of the Rising was led by whichever family held the most rope, and the rope-songs call these families the rope-holders. The rope-songs do not name any single rope-holder, and the Cord Archive holds no list of them. The people of the Drift today point to the rope-holders of the Rising as the reason the Drift has never had a monarch: the rope-songs remember the rule of the rope-holders as hard and unfair.

The rope-songs say that the people of the Rising already feared heights. One of the oldest rope-songs, called The Last Climb, tells of a child during the Rising who looked over the edge of an island and was struck dumb for a year. The Last Climb ends with a line that every child of the Drift still learns: "Look to the hearth and not to the edge." The fear of heights among the people of the Drift is therefore older than the calendar itself.

The rope-songs also say that the people of the Rising already spun cord. The Last Climb and several other old songs describe the people of the Rising throwing ropes from island to island whenever two islands drifted close, and hauling baskets of goods across on those ropes before the islands drifted apart again. The Rope-Scribes of the Cord Archive believe that the knot-records of the Drift began during the Rising as simple counting cords, although no such cord survives.

The Rising ended with the Lowering. The Lowering was the time when the islands of the Drift stopped their rise and settled in the clouds. The rope-songs say that the islands stopped climbing, dipped a short way downward, and then came to rest and did not move again. Because the islands dipped as they settled, the people of the Drift called this event the Lowering, and the AL calendar begins with the year of the Lowering, which is AL 1.

The islands did not all settle at the same height. Most of the islands of the Drift dipped only a little at the Lowering before coming to rest. The seven islands now called Low Drift dipped much farther and came to rest lowest of all. The rope-songs say that the people of those seven islands wept when their islands sank, believing the islands would fall forever, and that they rejoiced when the islands stopped.

After the Lowering, the islands of the Drift no longer moved apart and together. The distance between any two islands stayed the same from year to year, and for the first time bridges and cables could be built that would last. The rope-songs call this change "the tightening of the rope," and the people of the Drift remember the Lowering as the moment their history truly began.

What lies beneath the clouds is not known. No rope-song from the Rising describes what lies beneath the clouds, and no knot-record from any later era describes it either. The people of the Drift did not look over the edge during the Rising, and they have not looked since. This book does not describe what lies beneath the clouds, because no one in the Drift knows.
CHAPTER 8: THE SETTLING

The Settling is the first era of the Drift's recorded history. The Settling lasted from AL 1, the year of the Lowering, until AL 87, the year before the Salt War began. During the Settling, the people of the Drift built the first lasting bridges, cut the first terraces, strung the Long Tether, and founded the Council of Aubade.

The first work of the Settling was building bridges. Once the islands stopped moving after the Lowering, the people of the Drift began to build rope bridges between neighboring islands. The first bridges of the Settling had no woven sides, and the knot-records say that few people would cross them. The first bridge with woven sides and a woven roof was built between the First Pennon and the Second Pennon in AL 5, and after that every bridge in the Drift was built with woven sides.

The first terrace on Orrin's Shelf was cut in AL 3. The terrace was cut by the farmer Orrin Kell, who found that lichen-barley would grow in the cool air of the tilted island to the north of Kestra. The farmers who followed Orrin Kell cut a new terrace every few years, and by the end of the Settling all nine terraces of Orrin's Shelf were in use.

The Long Tether was first strung in AL 9. The first Long Tether was a thinner cable than the Long Tether of today, and only small cable-barges could travel along it. The weavers of Thrum twisted the first Long Tether from more than a thousand lengths of tetherweed rope, and the knot-records say that stringing the first Long Tether took two summers. From AL 9 onward, lichen-barley from Orrin's Shelf could reach Kestra in every season.

The Council of Aubade was founded in AL 12. Before AL 12, each island of the Drift settled its own affairs, and disputes between islands were settled by whoever had the most rope and the most people. In AL 12, the people of Kestra, Orrin's Shelf, and the Pennons agreed to govern their shared affairs through a single elected council meeting in Aubade. The founding of the Council of Aubade is counted as the founding of the Kestrine Drift as one realm.

The Council of Aubade had twelve seats at its founding. Kestra held six seats, Orrin's Shelf held three seats, and the Pennons held three seats. Salt Kettle and Low Drift held no seats on the first Council. The Brinewarden of Salt Kettle was invited to join the Council in AL 12 and refused, saying that Salt Kettle would sell its salt to the Council but would not be ruled by it.

The first First Speaker of the Council of Aubade was Maud Tarrow. Maud Tarrow was a weaver of Wefting on Kestra. Maud Tarrow was elected First Speaker in AL 12, the year the Council of Aubade was founded, at the age of 41. The Council chose Maud Tarrow because she had already spent many years settling quarrels between the weavers of Thrum and the weavers of Wefting, and because she was trusted by the Pennon families. Maud Tarrow served as First Speaker until AL 30.

The Council under Maud Tarrow made the first laws of the Drift. The first law tied in the Hall of Cords was the law forbidding any person to soak tetherweed in a drinking cistern. The second law set the term of a First Speaker at nine years. The third law made the brink walls of Kestra the shared duty of every household on Kestra.

The money of the Drift was first woven in AL 20. Before AL 20, the people of the Drift traded goods for goods. In AL 20, the Council of Aubade ordered the weavers of Wefting to weave Council cord of red and grey thread, to be cut into knots and wound into spools and bolts. From AL 20 onward, twenty knots made one spool and six spools made one bolt.

The prison on Orrin's Step was opened in AL 40. Before AL 40, each island punished its own wrongdoers, and a person who broke the law of one island could escape by crossing a bridge to another. In AL 40, the Council of Aubade named Orrin's Step as the single prison of the Drift and appointed the first Step-keeper to guard it.

The chain of bridges through the Pennons was completed in AL 55. By AL 55, all forty Pennons were joined together by rope bridges, from the First Pennon to the Fortieth, and a traveler could walk the whole chain from end to end. The Pennon families celebrated the completion of the chain with a festival that lasted nine days, and the rope-songs call AL 55 "the year the chain was closed."

Salt Kettle traded peacefully with the rest of the Drift for most of the Settling. The salt-boilers of Salt Kettle sold their salt at the Midspan of the Brine Bridge, and porters carried it onward to the other islands. The Brinewarden of Salt Kettle set the price of salt alone, and the Council of Aubade had no say in it. As the Settling drew to its end, the Council and the Brinewarden quarreled more and more often over that price, and the quarrel led to the Salt War.
CHAPTER 9: THE SALT WAR

The Salt War was the great war of the Kestrine Drift. The Salt War began in AL 88 and ended in AL 101. The Salt War was fought between Salt Kettle on one side and the Council of Aubade, with Kestra, Orrin's Shelf, and the Pennons, on the other side. The Salt War is the only war in the history of the Drift.

The Salt War began because only one island in the Drift has salt, and that island stopped trading. Salt Kettle holds the only brine spring in the Drift, so every grain of salt in the Drift comes from Salt Kettle. In AL 88, the Brinewarden of Salt Kettle, Harl Quenby, stopped all trade in salt. Harl Quenby closed the Brine Bridge at the Midspan, and no salt left Salt Kettle.

Harl Quenby gave two reasons for stopping the trade in salt. The first reason was that Salt Kettle held no seat on the Council of Aubade, although the whole Drift depended on its salt. The second reason was that the Kettle had run weak in AL 87, and Harl Quenby feared that the salt-boilers of Salt Kettle would starve if they sold salt for knots and then could not buy grain. Harl Quenby declared that Salt Kettle would keep its salt until the Council granted Salt Kettle a voice.

The Council of Aubade refused Harl Quenby's demand. The First Speaker of the Council in AL 88 was Dorran Pike, a councillor of Kestra. Dorran Pike argued that the Council could not be forced into granting seats by an island that withheld food from the whole Drift. The Council voted to stop sending lichen-barley across the Brine Bridge until Salt Kettle reopened the trade in salt.

The Salt War was not fought with armies on open ground. The Drift has no open ground between islands, and no one in the Drift flies. The Salt War was fought on and around the Brine Bridge. Bridge-wardens loyal to the Council held the Fortieth Pennon, and salt-boilers loyal to Harl Quenby held the Salt Kettle end of the Brine Bridge. For most of the war, the two sides faced one another across the Brine Bridge, and neither side could cross.

The Salt War brought hunger to both sides. Without salt, the people of Kestra, Orrin's Shelf, and the Pennons could not keep food through the cold months, and the knot-records of the Cord Archive record hunger on Kestra in many winters of the war. Without lichen-barley, the salt-boilers of Salt Kettle lived on stored grain, on eggs, and on what little the Pennon porters would smuggle across by night. Both sides suffered, and neither side would give way.

The worst event of the Salt War was the burning of the Midspan in AL 93. In AL 93, a band of salt-boilers crept along the Brine Bridge by night and set fire to the Midspan, hoping to stop the Council's bridge-wardens from ever crossing. The fire spread along the woven walls of the bridge, and the middle of the Brine Bridge fell away. No one could walk between Salt Kettle and the rest of the Drift again until the Midspan was rebuilt in AL 100.

In AL 95, Ivo Rennick became First Speaker of the Council of Aubade. Ivo Rennick was a councillor of the Pennons and a bridge-warden by trade. Speaker Ivo Rennick believed that the Salt War could not be won by waiting, and that the Council must offer Salt Kettle a place in the government of the Drift. Ivo Rennick sent word to Salt Kettle by a line thrown across the gap where the Midspan had stood.

In AL 97, the salt-boilers of Seethe removed Harl Quenby as Brinewarden and chose Sefa Oll in his place. Sefa Oll was a salt-boiler of Seethe who had argued throughout the war that Salt Kettle should make peace. Brinewarden Sefa Oll answered Ivo Rennick's message, and the two began to trade messages across the gap in the Brine Bridge by knotted cord.

The talks between Speaker Ivo Rennick and Brinewarden Sefa Oll went on for several years. The bridge-wardens of the Pennons and the salt-boilers of Salt Kettle rebuilt the Midspan together while the talks continued, and the rebuilt Midspan was finished in AL 100. The Midspan was rebuilt wider than before so that the leaders of both sides could stand on it together.

The Salt War ended with the Tether Compact in AL 101. The Tether Compact was signed on the rebuilt Midspan of the Brine Bridge by Speaker Ivo Rennick for the Council of Aubade and by Brinewarden Sefa Oll for Salt Kettle. The Tether Compact takes its name from the way it was recorded: the terms were tied as knots in a single long cord, the Tether Cord, and each leader tied the final knot at one end.

The Tether Compact set four terms. First, Salt Kettle would reopen the trade in salt and never close it again. Second, Salt Kettle would hold two seats on the Council of Aubade. Third, the price of salt would be set each year by the Council of Aubade and the Brinewarden together. Fourth, the Council would send lichen-barley to Salt Kettle in every season, even in years of poor harvest.

Harl Quenby was sent to Orrin's Step after the Salt War. The Tether Compact pardoned every salt-boiler and every bridge-warden who had fought in the Salt War, with one exception. The Tether Compact named Harl Quenby as the one person to be held to account, and Harl Quenby spent the rest of his life as a prisoner on Orrin's Step.

The Tether Cord is kept in the Hall of Cords to this day. The Tether Cord hangs at the center of the Hall of Cords, and every new First Speaker and every new Brinewarden touches the Tether Cord on the day they take office. The people of the Drift call the day the Tether Compact was signed Compact Day, and on Compact Day each year the Brinewarden and the First Speaker meet on the Midspan and exchange a cake of salt for a loaf of lichen-barley bread.
CHAPTER 10: THE KNOTTED YEARS

The Knotted Years were the long peace that followed the Salt War. The Knotted Years lasted from AL 101, the year of the Tether Compact, until AL 213, the year before the Great Gale. The Knotted Years take their name from the great number of knot-records tied during this era. More knot-records survive from the Knotted Years than from all the other eras of the Drift together.

The Cord Archive of Aubade was founded in AL 104. Before AL 104, knot-records were kept by whoever had tied them, and many knot-records from the Settling were lost or unraveled. In AL 104, the Council of Aubade built the Cord Archive beside the Hall of Cords and named the first Rope-Scribes to keep it. The first duty of the first Rope-Scribes was to gather every surviving knot-record of the Settling and the Salt War into the Cord Archive.

The first Head Rope-Scribe of the Cord Archive was Hesk Ambry. Hesk Ambry was a weaver of Wefting who had kept the knot-records of the Council during the Salt War. Hesk Ambry served as Head Rope-Scribe from the founding of the Cord Archive in AL 104 until AL 131, and in those years Hesk Ambry sorted the surviving knot-records of the Settling into the order in which the Cord Archive still keeps them.

The Rope-Scribes of the Knotted Years developed the full knot-script of the Drift. Before the Knotted Years, a knot-record could hold only numbers and a few simple signs. The Rope-Scribes of the Knotted Years added knots for the names of islands, for the names of offices such as First Speaker and Brinewarden, and for many common words. By the end of the Knotted Years, a skilled Rope-Scribe could tie a whole law, a whole letter, or a whole rope-song in knotted cord.

The Guild of Wardens was founded in AL 120. Before AL 120, the bridge-wardens of the Pennons trained their own apprentices in their own ways, and the knots of one bridge might not match the knots of the next. In AL 120, the bridge-wardens of all forty Pennons met on the Ninth Pennon and founded the Guild of Wardens, which set one standard of knots and one course of training for every bridge-warden in the Drift. The Guild Hall of the Guild of Wardens still stands on the Ninth Pennon.

The poet Liesl Marro lived during the Knotted Years. Liesl Marro came from the Twelfth Pennon and worked as a porter on the Pennon bridges before becoming known as a poet. Liesl Marro gathered the old rope-songs of the Pennons, and in AL 156 Liesl Marro completed Songs from the Rope, the most famous book of poems in the Drift. Songs from the Rope was the first book of poems to be tied in full by the Rope-Scribes of the Cord Archive.

Low Drift joined the Council of Aubade in AL 163. The stone-cutters of Low Drift had asked for a seat on the Council several times during the Knotted Years, and each time the councillors of Kestra had answered that Kestra already spoke for Low Drift. In AL 163, the Council voted to grant Low Drift one seat of its own. With the seat of Low Drift, the Council of Aubade reached fifteen seats, the number it holds today.

The Long Tether was rewoven in AL 180. The first Long Tether, strung during the Settling, had grown thin and worn by the Knotted Years, and only small cable-barges could safely travel it. In AL 180, the weavers of Thrum twisted a new and much thicker Long Tether, and the new Long Tether could carry cable-barges twice the size of the old ones. The trade in lichen-barley between Orrin's Shelf and Kestra grew greatly after AL 180.

The trade between the islands grew throughout the Knotted Years. Salt from Salt Kettle, grain from Orrin's Shelf, cloth and cord from Kestra, stone and lamp-moss from Low Drift, and eggs and baskets from the Pennons all passed between the islands along the bridges and the Long Tether. The knot-records of the Knotted Years show more trade in each generation than in the one before.

The Council of Aubade passed the Brink Law in AL 190. The Brink Law required every island in the Drift to wall its brinks in the manner of Kestra, with cloudstone at the bottom and woven screens at the top. Before the Brink Law, the smaller islands had walled only the brinks near their houses. After the Brink Law, the brinks of every island in the Drift were walled from end to end.

The Knotted Years were not free of trouble. The knot-records of the Knotted Years record three poor harvests on Orrin's Shelf, several bridge failures in the Pennons, and a long quarrel between the weavers of Thrum and the weavers of Wefting over the price of rope. None of these troubles led to fighting. The Rope-Scribes of the Cord Archive count the Knotted Years as the first long peace in the history of the Drift.

The Knotted Years ended with the Great Gale. In the last years of the Knotted Years, the knot-records note stronger winds than usual across the Pennons, and several bridge-wardens warned the Council that the bridges were straining. The Council of Aubade ordered the bridges to be strengthened, but the work was not finished when the Great Gale struck in AL 214.
CHAPTER 11: THE GREAT GALE

The Great Gale was the worst disaster in the history of the Kestrine Drift. The Great Gale struck in AL 214. The Great Gale was a storm of wind so strong that it tore roofs from houses across the Drift, broke bridges in the Pennons, and snapped the Long Tether. The people of the Drift still date events as happening "before the Gale" or "after the Gale."

The Great Gale came from the west. The knot-records of the Cord Archive say that the Great Gale began in the early afternoon, when a wall of dark cloud rose over the western brinks of the Drift. The Great Gale blew for three days and three nights without stopping. The Rope-Scribes on duty in the Cord Archive tied a knot for each hour of the storm, and that cord, called the Gale Cord, is still kept in the Cord Archive.

The Great Gale snapped the Long Tether. On the second night of the Great Gale, the Long Tether broke near its Kestran end, and the whole length of the Long Tether fell away from the Tether-post. The Long Tether was the only way to reach Orrin's Shelf, so when the Long Tether snapped, Orrin's Shelf was cut off from the rest of the Drift. One cable-barge was on the Long Tether when it broke, and the crew and passengers of that cable-barge were lost.

The Great Gale did great harm in the Pennons. The Pennons are small and light, and the Great Gale swung the whole chain back and forth until the ropes of several bridges parted. Eleven bridges between the Pennons broke during the Great Gale, and the families on the Pennons between the broken bridges were cut off from one another for many weeks. The bridge-wardens of the Guild of Wardens worked through the storm to hold the remaining bridges together.

Low Drift suffered least of all the islands in the Great Gale. The seven islands of Low Drift lie beneath the southern brink of Kestra, and Kestra sheltered them from the western wind. The stone-cutters of Low Drift lost no houses in the Great Gale, because the houses of Chisel are roofed with cloudstone slabs rather than tetherweed thatch. After the Great Gale, the stone-cutters of Low Drift climbed the Walled Stair to help the people of Aubade rebuild.

Salt Kettle lost the roofs of its boiling-houses in the Great Gale. The wind blew out every fire under the pans of Seethe and scattered the drying salt across the bowl of the island. The salt-boilers of Salt Kettle relit their fires on the fourth day, as soon as the wind dropped, and the Brinewarden of the time sent salt across the Brine Bridge within the week. The knot-records of the Cord Archive note that Salt Kettle kept its promise under the Tether Compact even in the worst year the Drift had known.

The Great Gale damaged Aubade as well. The Great Gale tore the tetherweed roofs from most of the houses of Aubade and tore away part of the roof of the Hall of Cords. Many law-cords hanging in the Hall of Cords were blown loose, and the Rope-Scribes spent the following year finding them, sorting them, and hanging them again. The Tether Cord of the Tether Compact was not lost, because a Rope-Scribe named Wim Adderley held it through the second night of the storm.

The Cord Archive records that about four hundred people of the Drift died in the Great Gale. Most of those who died were in the Pennons, where houses and bridges suffered most, and on the Long Tether. The Cord Archive holds a memorial cord for those who died in the Great Gale, with one knot for each person lost, and the memorial cord hangs beside the Gale Cord.

After the Great Gale, Orrin's Shelf lived on the Deep Granary. With the Long Tether snapped, the farmers of Orrin's Shelf could not send grain to Kestra and could not receive salt, cord, or lamp-moss. The farmers of Orrin's Shelf opened the Deep Granary and fed themselves on stored lichen-barley. The law of Orrin's Shelf that the Deep Granary must be kept at least half full proved its worth in the months after the Great Gale.

The rest of the Drift went hungry after the Great Gale. Lichen-barley grows only on Orrin's Shelf, so with the Long Tether snapped, no grain reached Kestra, the Pennons, Salt Kettle, or Low Drift. The Council of Aubade opened its own grain stores and shared them out by household, and the people of the Drift ate eggs, greens, and salted food laid by in earlier seasons. The hungry time that followed the Great Gale is remembered as the Hungry Winter, although it lasted through more than one winter, until grain crossed again in AL 216.

The First Speaker of the Council of Aubade during the Great Gale was Bettany Loame. Bettany Loame was a councillor of Kestra and a rain-keeper by trade. First Speaker Bettany Loame kept the Council meeting through the three days of the Great Gale in the half-roofed Hall of Cords, and she ordered the sharing out of the Council's grain stores on the first morning after the storm. Bettany Loame is remembered as the First Speaker who held the Drift together through the Gale.

The prisoners of Orrin's Step survived the Great Gale. Orrin's Step lies close beneath Orrin's Shelf, and the Shelf sheltered the small rock from the worst of the wind. The Step-keeper went on sending food down the Cage Ladder throughout the Great Gale and the months that followed, drawing on the Deep Granary of Orrin's Shelf.

The Great Gale ended the Knotted Years and began a new era. With the Long Tether snapped, eleven Pennon bridges broken, and Aubade half unroofed, the Council of Aubade faced the task of binding the Drift back together. The work of repairing the Drift after the Great Gale gave the next era its name, the Rebinding.
CHAPTER 12: THE REBINDING

The Rebinding is the era of the Drift that began after the Great Gale and continues today. The Rebinding began in AL 215 and continues to AL 340, the year this book was written. The Rebinding takes its name from the work of binding the islands of the Drift back together after the Great Gale broke them apart. The chief work of the Rebinding was restoring the Long Tether.

The first task of the Rebinding was to carry a line from Kestra to Orrin's Shelf. With the Long Tether snapped, there was no way to cross from Kestra to Orrin's Shelf, and no one in the Drift flies. In AL 215, the weavers of Wefting built a great kite of tetherweed cloth and flew it from the Tether-post of Kestra on a south wind, trailing a thin cord behind it. On the fourth attempt, the kite came down on the terraces of Orrin's Shelf, and the farmers of Orrin's Shelf caught the cord and made it fast.

The cord carried by the kite was called the Thin Line. The Thin Line was too thin to carry anything but a heavier cord, so the people of Kestra tied a heavier cord to the Thin Line and the farmers of Orrin's Shelf hauled it across. Each heavier cord was used to haul a heavier one after it. The people of the Drift still use the phrase "the Thin Line" for any small beginning that leads to something great.

Grain first crossed from Orrin's Shelf to Kestra after the Great Gale in AL 216. Once a rope strong enough to carry a small basket had been hauled across, the farmers of Orrin's Shelf began sending sacks of lichen-barley along it one at a time, and the Hungry Winter came to an end. The knot-records say that the first sack of grain to cross after the Great Gale was carried to the Hall of Cords and opened before the whole Council.

The Long Tether was restored in AL 221. The weavers of Thrum twisted the new Long Tether from tetherweed rope, the stone-cutters of Low Drift rebuilt the Tether-post, and the new Long Tether was hauled across along the ropes that had followed the Thin Line. The restored Long Tether was thicker than any Long Tether before it, and it has not broken since. Cable-barges began traveling the Long Tether again in AL 221.

The bridges of the Pennons were rebuilt during the early Rebinding. The Guild of Wardens rebuilt the eleven Pennon bridges broken by the Great Gale, and by AL 219 the chain of the Pennons was whole again. The rebuilt bridges were given double ropes on their windward sides, and every Pennon bridge built since the Great Gale has been built the same way.

The Council of Aubade passed the Gale Laws in AL 230. The Gale Laws required every island to keep a store of grain, salt, and lamp-moss large enough to last one winter without trade. The Gale Laws also required the Guild of Wardens to inspect every bridge in the Drift after any storm, and required a spare line to be kept coiled at the Tether-post of Kestra at all times. The Gale Laws are the most important laws passed during the Rebinding.

The cisterns of the Drift were enlarged during the Rebinding. The Great Gale had broken many cistern covers and fouled much of the water of the Drift, and in the years after the Gale many households went short of water. During the Rebinding, the Council of Aubade paid the stone-cutters of Low Drift to cut new cistern covers and to enlarge the Great Cistern of Aubade. By AL 260, the Great Cistern of Aubade held half again as much water as it had held before the Great Gale.

The Rebinding has been a time of peace and slow growth. The knot-records of the Rebinding show no war, no great famine after the Hungry Winter, and no storm to match the Great Gale. The number of households in the Drift has grown in every generation of the Rebinding, and new houses have been built on the Pennons, in Chisel on Low Drift, and in the villages of Kestra.

The present First Speaker of the Council of Aubade is Corra Fenwick. Corra Fenwick is a councillor of Salt Kettle, and Corra Fenwick is the first First Speaker from Salt Kettle in the history of the Drift. Corra Fenwick was elected First Speaker in AL 334. The election of a First Speaker from Salt Kettle is counted by many in the Drift as the final healing of the Salt War.

The present Brinewarden of Salt Kettle is Oda Thresh. Oda Thresh has served as Brinewarden since AL 329. Each year on Compact Day, Brinewarden Oda Thresh and First Speaker Corra Fenwick meet on the Midspan of the Brine Bridge and exchange a cake of salt for a loaf of lichen-barley bread, as every Brinewarden and First Speaker has done since the Tether Compact.

The Drift in AL 340 is whole. The Long Tether is whole again, the chain of the Pennons is closed, and the Walled Stair and the Stone Hoist join Kestra to Low Drift. The Council of Aubade holds fifteen seats, the Cord Archive holds more knot-records than ever before, and the people of the Drift still keep their eyes inward. This is the Drift that this book records.
PART THREE: NOTABLE FIGURES

CHAPTER 13: NOTABLE FIGURES

This chapter describes the notable figures of the Kestrine Drift, in the order in which they appear in the history of the Drift. Each entry names the person, the island the person came from, and the reason the person is remembered. Where the Cord Archive records a year, the year is given. Where the Cord Archive does not record a year, no year is given.

Orrin Kell was the farmer who cut the first terrace on Orrin's Shelf. Orrin Kell came from Kestra and crossed to the tilted island north of Kestra in the first years after the Lowering. Orrin Kell cut the first terrace in AL 3, and Orrin Kell was the first person in the Drift to grow lichen-barley on purpose rather than gathering it wild. Orrin's Shelf is named for Orrin Kell, and so is the prison rock of Orrin's Step below it. The highest terrace of Orrin's Shelf is called Kell's Terrace in Orrin Kell's memory.

Maud Tarrow was the first First Speaker of the Council of Aubade. Maud Tarrow was a weaver of Wefting on Kestra. Maud Tarrow was elected First Speaker in AL 12, the year the Council of Aubade was founded, at the age of 41. Maud Tarrow served as First Speaker until AL 30. Under Maud Tarrow, the Council tied the first laws of the Drift in the Hall of Cords and, in AL 20, ordered the first Council cord to be woven for money. Maud Tarrow is remembered as the founder of the government of the Drift, and a cord said to have been tied by Maud Tarrow's own hands hangs near the door of the Hall of Cords.

Maud Tarrow was known for settling quarrels. Before becoming First Speaker, Maud Tarrow spent many years settling disputes between the weavers of Thrum and the weavers of Wefting, who quarreled over the price and quality of rope. Maud Tarrow's method was to have both sides tie their demands in a single cord and then untie, knot by knot, every demand that the other side could accept. The people of the Drift still call this method "a Tarrow untying."

Harl Quenby was the Brinewarden of Salt Kettle who began the Salt War. Harl Quenby was a salt-boiler of Seethe on Salt Kettle, chosen as Brinewarden before the Salt War. In AL 88, Harl Quenby stopped all trade in salt and closed the Brine Bridge, demanding a voice for Salt Kettle on the Council of Aubade. Harl Quenby was removed as Brinewarden by the salt-boilers of Seethe in AL 97. Under the Tether Compact, Harl Quenby was the one person not pardoned for the Salt War, and Harl Quenby spent the rest of his life as a prisoner on Orrin's Step.

Harl Quenby is remembered in two ways. On Kestra and on Orrin's Shelf, Harl Quenby is remembered as the man who starved the Drift for salt. On Salt Kettle, Harl Quenby is remembered more kindly, as the Brinewarden who won Salt Kettle its seats on the Council, even though he did not live free to see them used. Both memories are recorded in the Cord Archive, and this book does not choose between them.

Dorran Pike was the First Speaker of the Council of Aubade when the Salt War began. Dorran Pike was a councillor of Kestra. In AL 88, First Speaker Dorran Pike refused Harl Quenby's demand for Council seats and led the Council in stopping the trade in lichen-barley to Salt Kettle. Dorran Pike served as First Speaker until AL 95.

Ivo Rennick was the First Speaker of the Council of Aubade who ended the Salt War. Ivo Rennick was a councillor of the Pennons and a bridge-warden by trade. Ivo Rennick became First Speaker in AL 95 and began talks with Salt Kettle by a line thrown across the gap in the Brine Bridge. Speaker Ivo Rennick signed the Tether Compact on the Midspan of the Brine Bridge in AL 101, together with Brinewarden Sefa Oll. Ivo Rennick was the first First Speaker to come from the Pennons.

Sefa Oll was the Brinewarden of Salt Kettle who made peace. Sefa Oll was a salt-boiler of Seethe who argued for peace throughout the Salt War. The salt-boilers of Seethe chose Sefa Oll as Brinewarden in AL 97, after they removed Harl Quenby. Brinewarden Sefa Oll signed the Tether Compact on the Midspan of the Brine Bridge in AL 101, together with Speaker Ivo Rennick. After the Tether Compact, Sefa Oll took one of the two new Council seats of Salt Kettle and was the first councillor from Salt Kettle ever to sit in the Hall of Cords.

Liesl Marro was the most famous poet of the Kestrine Drift. Liesl Marro came from the Twelfth Pennon and lived during the Knotted Years. Liesl Marro worked as a porter on the Pennon bridges as a young person and learned the rope-songs from the other porters and the bridge-wardens. Liesl Marro gathered the rope-songs of the Pennons, reshaped them, and added new poems of Liesl Marro's own. In AL 156, Liesl Marro completed Songs from the Rope.

Songs from the Rope is the most famous book of poems in the Drift. Songs from the Rope holds poems about the Rising, the Lowering, the building of the bridges, and the Salt War, as well as poems about ordinary life on the Pennons. The best-known poem in Songs from the Rope is The Warden's Morning, which describes a bridge-warden testing every knot of a bridge at dawn. Songs from the Rope is recited on the Pennons on the first evening of every year.

Bettany Loame was the First Speaker of the Council of Aubade during the Great Gale. Bettany Loame was a councillor of Kestra and a rain-keeper by trade. First Speaker Bettany Loame kept the Council meeting in the half-roofed Hall of Cords through the three days of the Great Gale in AL 214, and on the first morning after the storm Bettany Loame ordered the Council's grain stores to be shared out by household. Bettany Loame is remembered as the First Speaker who held the Drift together through the Great Gale and the Hungry Winter that followed.

Wim Adderley was a Rope-Scribe of the Cord Archive during the Great Gale. When the Great Gale tore away part of the roof of the Hall of Cords in AL 214, Wim Adderley held the Tether Cord of the Tether Compact through the second night of the storm so that it would not be blown away. The Tether Cord still hangs in the Hall of Cords because of Wim Adderley. Every new Rope-Scribe of the Cord Archive learns the story of Wim Adderley on their first day.

Corra Fenwick is the present First Speaker of the Council of Aubade. Corra Fenwick is a councillor of Salt Kettle and was elected First Speaker in AL 334. Corra Fenwick is the first First Speaker from Salt Kettle in the history of the Drift. Corra Fenwick was a salt-boiler of Seethe before entering the Council, and is known in the Hall of Cords for plain speech and short meetings.

Oda Thresh is the present Brinewarden of Salt Kettle. Oda Thresh has served as Brinewarden since AL 329. Oda Thresh is known on Salt Kettle for having rebuilt the oldest boiling-houses of Seethe and for having set the price of salt together with the Council every year without a single dispute.

Tamsin Vey, the author of this book, is a Rope-Scribe of the Cord Archive of Aubade. Tamsin Vey has served as Rope-Scribe since AL 322 and wrote this book in AL 340. Tamsin Vey does not count as a notable figure of the Drift, and is listed here only so that a reader may know who tied these words.

APPENDIX B: GLOSSARY

This glossary explains words a visitor to the Kestrine Drift will hear. The words are listed in alphabetical order.

AL: The calendar of the Drift. AL stands for "After the Lowering." AL 1 is the year the islands stopped their rise and settled in the clouds.
Anvil: The largest island of Low Drift, where the village of Chisel stands.
Aubade: The capital of the Kestrine Drift, on the island of Kestra, where the Council of Aubade meets.
Bolt: The largest unit of money in the Drift. One bolt is six spools.
Bridge-warden: A keeper of a rope bridge, who inspects its ropes and reties its knots. Bridge-wardens belong to the Guild of Wardens.
Brine Bridge: The rope bridge that joins Salt Kettle to the rest of the Drift.
Brinewarden: The keeper of the Kettle and the leader of Salt Kettle, chosen by the salt-boilers of Seethe.
Brink: The edge of an island.
Brink Law: The law of AL 190 requiring every island to wall its brinks.
Brinkward: A Low Drift word for anything that faces an edge. To call a person brinkward is an insult.
Cable-barge: A covered wooden car that hangs from a great cable and is wound along it by its crew.
Cage Ladder: The enclosed ladder that hangs from the lowest terrace of Orrin's Shelf down to Orrin's Step.
Chisel: The main village of Low Drift, on the Anvil.
Clearing: The festival of Low Drift, held each year on the first morning when the mist lifts from all seven islands at once.
Cloudstone: The pale grey building stone quarried on Low Drift.
Compact Day: The yearly day when the Brinewarden and the First Speaker meet on the Midspan to remember the Tether Compact.
Cooling: The festival of Salt Kettle, the one day each year when every salt pan is left cold.
Cord Archive: The keeping-place of knot-records in Aubade, founded in AL 104.
Council cord: The red-and-grey cord, woven only in Wefting, that is used for money.
Council of Aubade: The elected council that governs the Kestrine Drift, led by a First Speaker.
Deep Granary: The stone cellars of Orrin's Shelf where lichen-barley is stored.
Eyes inward: A saying of the Drift, taught to every child, meaning "never look over the edge."
First Speaker: The leader of the Council of Aubade, elected by the councillors for a term of nine years.
Furrowend: The village of the terrace farmers on Orrin's Shelf.
Gale Laws: The laws of AL 230 passed after the Great Gale.
Great Gale: The storm of AL 214 that snapped the Long Tether.
Hall of Cords: The building in Aubade where the Council meets and where the laws of the Drift hang as knotted cords.
Hungry Winter: The hungry time after the Great Gale, lasting until grain crossed again in AL 216, when no grain reached Kestra.
Kestra Span: The rope bridge that joins the First Pennon to Kestra.
Kettle: The brine spring of Salt Kettle, the only brine spring in the Drift.
Knot: The smallest unit of money in the Drift, a measured length of Council cord tied off at both ends.
Knot-record: A record tied in knotted cord instead of written in ink.
Lamp-moss: An oily moss from Low Drift, dried and burned for light and heat.
Lichen-barley: The only grain of the Drift, grown only on Orrin's Shelf.
Long Tether: The great cable between Kestra and Orrin's Shelf, along which cable-barges travel.
Lowering: The event that ended the Rising, when the islands stopped their rise and settled in the clouds.
Midspan: The wide platform at the middle of the Brine Bridge, where the Tether Compact was signed.
Night of Loose Ends: The festival of Kestra on the last night of each year.
Rain-keeper: The guardian of a cistern.
Rising: The time before the Lowering, when the islands were still climbing through the clouds.
Rope-Scribe: A keeper of knot-records in the Cord Archive.
Rope-song: A work song of the Pennons, sung in time with the pull of a rope.
Seethe: The only town on Salt Kettle.
Sheaf Night: The harvest festival of Orrin's Shelf.
Spool: A unit of money in the Drift. One spool is twenty-four knots.
Step-keeper: The keeper of the prison on Orrin's Step.
Stone Hoist: The great wheel on the southern brink of Kestra that lifts cloudstone from Low Drift.
Tether Compact: The agreement of AL 101 that ended the Salt War.
Tether Cord: The knotted cord that records the Tether Compact, kept in the Hall of Cords.
Tether-post: The cloudstone pillar on the northern brink of Kestra where the Long Tether is fixed.
Tetherweed: The grey-green fiber plant of Kestra from which cord, rope, and cloth are made.
Thin Line: The cord carried by kite from Kestra to Orrin's Shelf in AL 215. Also, any small beginning that leads to something great.
Thrum: The weaving village of western Kestra, known for heavy rope.
Walled Stair: The enclosed stairway that joins Kestra to Low Drift.
Warden's hitch: The load-bearing knot of the bridge-wardens, taught only within the Guild of Wardens.
Wefting: The weaving village of eastern Kestra, the only place where Council cord is woven.
